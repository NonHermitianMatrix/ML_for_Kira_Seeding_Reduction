\begin{abstract}
Feynman integral reduction expresses the loop integrals in a particle-physics calculation through a smaller set of master integrals. We studied how machine learning and exact analytic identities can reduce the time required to complete this calculation with Kira. We first constructed 2.30 million targets with known master expansions and recorded seed and elimination data. We trained seed and row models, tested their algebraic sufficiency, and then evaluated their cost inside Kira. We then developed equation selectors and models of complete reduction time, combined them with selective seed enlargement and master-basis conversion, and added shared calculations and analytic reductions. Model gains depend on the workload: a shared structural model reduces time by 4.9\% against a matched fixed strategy on 43 completed native requests, while the row model gives no reliable retained gain. Exact recurrences give a much larger but narrower result. For a specified coupled-vacuum family at one through twelve loops, a direct comparison reduces total time from 1,027.781 to 1.922 seconds. Later direct formulas and coefficient reconstruction reduce a separate 75-request workload from 81.827 to 49.852 seconds. The final 93-request update gives a further, separately measured 8.8\% reduction against its preceding application. We explain the data, models, mathematical identities, controls, and unsuccessful approaches that led to these results. Restricted analytic families extend through thirteen loops; general high-loop scattering remains outside the demonstrated timing gains.
\end{abstract}
