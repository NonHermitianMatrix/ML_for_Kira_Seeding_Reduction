\begin{abstract}
We study the time required to decompose loop integrals into master integrals. The method combines exact analytic recurrences, independent-loop factorization, and learned choices in Kira. A scalar one-loop recurrence reduces raised propagator powers through small matrices of masses and momentum invariants, while the retained models select sparse seeds for supported coupled blocks. All coefficients remain exact. An initial 41-request test includes massive one-loop polygons, symbolic two-point kinematics, independent products through thirteen loops, and coupled compatibility cases. The combined application completes 41 requests in three repetitions. On 41 matched requests, its summed median time is 65.266 seconds, compared with 104.757 seconds for the matched native control, a factor of 1.61. The recurrence reduces time by 36.4\% on its 30 supported requests relative to the equally prepared native control. On eight coupled nonplanar requests, the retained models use 6.4\% less time than the exact fixed control, with repetition interval $[0.873,0.988]$. Two symbolic requests were slower, so the final application uses native reduction when the new recurrence would require symbolic setup. A separate nine-request test verifies this rule and measures 85.6\% less time on four symbolic requests relative to the initial analytic variant. The models remain unchanged, and earlier transfer regressions remain documented. Timings include application loading, decisions, algebra, exact checks, and recovery; master-index preparation remains separate. The high-loop product tests establish performance for factorable structures and do not establish a general solution for connected high-loop scattering.
\end{abstract}
