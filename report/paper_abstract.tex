\begin{abstract}
Integration-by-parts reduction expresses Feynman integrals in a master basis~\cite{KiraOriginal}. We train models to select seeds and equations before Kira performs exact algebra, and compare their complete reduction time with matched controls. Local seed and equation models give measured savings, while learned row selection gives no reliable gain and is omitted. To test transfer, we train one structural cost model while excluding whole families and all loop orders above three from fitting and model selection. A fresh grid defines 110 requests through twelve loops, of which 43 give matched nontrivial native reductions. Their learned-to-native time ratio is 0.900, and their learned-to-fixed ratio is 0.951. The 15 matched physical holdouts give a learned-to-native ratio of 0.939. Timings include prediction, equation generation, exact algebra, checks, and recovery, with master discovery reported separately. A separate 33-request preparation comparison trains on both completed and failed attempts and separates the cost of basis reuse from the effect of learned choices. It does not support learned preparation, so that stage retains native choices. Failed references and unsupported inputs remain explicit. The calculations keep the dimension symbolic and otherwise use rational kinematics. These results measure the supported domain of the learned method; they do not establish a speedup for every integral or loop order.
\end{abstract}
