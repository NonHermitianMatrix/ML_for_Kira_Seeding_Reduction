\begin{abstract}
We study how exact identities and machine learning reduce the time needed to express loop integrals in terms of master integrals with Kira. We first integrate supported subgraphs analytically and then use a model trained on complete reduction times to choose the remaining seed work. The exact identities cover connected massless bubble trees with two-point or one-scale triangle roots, and a new extension covers a single massive vacuum root. Their Gamma-function ratios reduce to finite rational products. The new vacuum rule completes 34 of 34 defined structures through thirteen loops. On 4 matched completed requests, time changes from 5.046 to 0.730 seconds. A separately retrained seed model uses 32 training and 16 validation requests. On 32 reserved native box requests, it takes 150.663 seconds against 156.383 seconds for fixed choices, with time ratio 0.963. A final application revision completes a separate 22-request test; its native part completes eight requests with the model and seven with fixed choices, and the ratio on the seven matched requests is 0.963. We separate analytic gains from learned gains, include application and recovery costs, and retain failures in the coverage results. Learned row selection remains omitted. The tested domains have explicit mass, kinematic, and numerator restrictions; the results do not establish a speedup for arbitrary high-loop scattering.
\end{abstract}
