\begin{abstract}
We study the time required to express loop integrals in terms of master integrals. The method combines exact formulas and recurrences with Kira reduction and learned choices based on measured complete cost. Direct partial fractions and connected-tree formulas provide analytic values for specified vacuum families through thirteen loops. For remaining native reductions, we retain the seed policy and compare symbolic algebra with finite-field reconstruction. A frozen comparison contains 22 new native requests and 53 previously verified analytic requests. The integrated application completes 75 of 75 requests, and its summed median time changes from 79.624 to 49.852 seconds on 75 matched completed inputs. The cumulative comparison across the direct vacuum and reconstruction changes gives 39.1\% less time.

Separate validation rejects the new algebra-cost model, so the retained method uses fixed reconstruction settings and the preceding seed model. We omit learned row selection because the earlier controls did not establish a saving. Exact coefficient checks support the tested reductions, while general multiscale high-loop scattering remains outside the demonstrated scope.
\end{abstract}
