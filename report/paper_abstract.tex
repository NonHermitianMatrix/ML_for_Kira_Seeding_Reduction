\begin{abstract}
We study the time required to decompose Feynman integrals using Kira, exact analytic identities, and machine learning. Recognized vacuum factors are evaluated by partial fractions and analytic tadpole formulas, while connected one-mass trees return explicit Gamma-function values. For supported remaining two-loop boxes, we train a measured-cost selector for sparse and complete seed regions, including failed attempts in its objective. Validation rejects the new complete-region selector, so the final application retains the preceding native seed model. A frozen comparison tests 69 requests, with vacuum products and connected trees at every loop order from one through thirteen. The integrated and preceding applications complete 69 and 69 requests. Their 69 matched requests take 109.273 and 111.950 seconds, respectively. On 16 matched native batches, the integrated-to-fixed time ratio is 1.002, with a 95\% interval of $[0.994,1.012]$. A separate analytic coverage grid completes 26 of 26 requests. We report analytic and learned effects separately and retain native row selection because earlier learned row policies did not establish a reliable benefit. The results apply to the declared domains and do not establish a speedup for every high-loop scattering integral.
\end{abstract}
