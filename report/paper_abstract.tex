\begin{abstract}
We study the time required to decompose Feynman integrals into master integrals using Kira, exact analytic identities, and machine learning. The method applies recognized exact reductions before native elimination and uses measured-cost models to select seed regions for supported remaining requests. We extend the exact stage to zero-invariant two-point sectors with polynomial numerators and unequal, equal, or zero masses, including their occurrence inside larger one-loop reductions. Scalar formulas also retain symbolic masses. A frozen comparison contains 68 requests, including independent products through thirteen loops and 24 native two-loop box batches. The integrated method completes 68 requests and the preceding application completes 68; their 68 matched requests take 213.813 and 252.923 seconds, respectively. On 24 matched native batches, learned selection takes 0.987 times the fixed-choice cost, with a 95\% interval of $[0.970,1.002]$. We report exact and learned effects separately and omit learned row selection because its earlier tests did not establish a reliable benefit. The results apply to the defined integral domains and do not establish a universal high-loop speedup.
\end{abstract}
