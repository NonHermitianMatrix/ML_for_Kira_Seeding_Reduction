\begin{abstract}
Feynman integral reduction expresses the many loop integrals in a particle-physics calculation through a smaller set of master integrals. We study how to shorten this step using Kira, exact analytic identities, and machine learning. The method first checks whether an integral has a direct formula or a recurrence that reduces its propagator powers. It sends the remaining targets to Kira, where measured reduction costs determine which integrals generate equations and how the coefficients are calculated. We test each added learned choice against a fixed procedure and retain it only when validation supports its use. The final comparison contains 93 requests, including connected two- and three-loop families and restricted analytic families through thirteen loops. Median time for the complete collection falls from 45.091 to 41.137 seconds relative to the preceding application, a reduction of 8.8\%. All 837 results in the three-method, three-repetition comparison pass the exact reference comparisons. Earlier seed and equation models give gains on their own test sets, but the added arithmetic selector and equation filter do not improve the final method and remain disabled. The results establish a faster combined procedure for the stated inputs. They leave general connected high-loop scattering and a reliable additional machine-learning gain in the final comparison unverified.
\end{abstract}
