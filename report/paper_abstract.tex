\begin{abstract}
We study how to reduce the time needed to decompose loop integrals into master integrals. The method combines direct analytic formulas, independent-loop factorization, exact scalar one-loop recurrences, and learned choices in Kira. A new cost-sensitive tree selects a recurrence strategy, while a retained seed model selects sparse native reductions within its validated domain. The algebra and output checks remain exact. An initial reserved test contains 65 requests, including vacuum and propagator families and independent products through thirteen loops. The initial candidate completes 65 requests in all three repetitions. Across 65 matched requests, its summed median time is 56.945 seconds, compared with 81.574 seconds for the preceding application, a factor of 1.43. The time ratio to the fast fixed control is 0.957, with repetition interval $[0.925,0.986]$. A separate bounded audit covers 110 requests at one through thirteen loops; the new application completes 68, and 62 paired results agree exactly with the preceding application. Full-application validation finds no resolved gain from the recurrence model, so the final workflow uses the fixed recurrence strategy and retains learned seed selection. A separate twelve-request test gives 1.56 times the speed of the preceding application, with all 12 final-method requests verified. Timings include loading, prediction, algebra, checks, and recovery. Master-index and reference preparation remain separate. The controls distinguish exact implementation savings from learned gains. General connected high-loop scattering and universal speedup remain outside the demonstrated coverage.
\end{abstract}
