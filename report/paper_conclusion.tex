\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
We combine exact subintegral integration with learned choices for the remaining Kira reduction. The exact stage uses proved identities within explicit domains, while the trained native policies choose seed and equation work using measured reduction costs. The solver retains responsibility for the algebra. Learned row selection remains omitted because its measured timing improvement is unresolved.

For the connected triangle trees, cancelling Gamma factors before finite expansion reduces the large-batch time from 14.305 to 6.731 seconds in the comparison of Section~\ref{sec:gamma-cancel}. The two-point extension completes all 34 defined connected structures through thirteen loops. On its 3 matched completed requests, time falls from 11.185 to 0.433 seconds (Table~\ref{tab:prop-tree}). These added gains come from exact algebra. Failed controls remain in the coverage counts and receive no finite speedup factor.

On the sixteen native box requests in the latest comparison, the retained ML policy takes 48.941 seconds against 52.226 seconds for fixed choices. Its time ratio is 0.937, with request interval $[0.853,1.010]$. This interval includes equal time, so this fresh sample does not resolve a reliable ML gain. Earlier controlled comparisons establish separate seed and equation-selection gains. The preceding forty-box experiment found 2.7\% less time from its added batch decision under its own timer. These results use different workloads and controls, so their speed factors cannot be multiplied into a universal gain.

The installed native build accepts at most 31 indices, including auxiliary scalar-product coordinates. Its loop limit therefore depends on the number of independent external momenta. The derived subintegral identities avoid that input for their supported families. The implementation and tests cover the stated analytic domains through thirteen loops; general massive, multiscale, and irreducible high-loop scattering remain outside the demonstrated coverage. The preceding broad grid still completes 68 of 110 requests. All reported gains apply to their defined comparisons and establish no globally minimum reduction time.
