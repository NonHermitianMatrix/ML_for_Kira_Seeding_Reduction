\section{Conclusion}
The reduction workflow can save time by removing exact work before selecting the remaining algebra. The triangle formula replaces equation generation and elimination within its stated one-scale domain. Independent-loop factorization extends that result to products through thirteen loops. For coupled two-loop boxes, the new model learns whether targets should share a reduction and which sparse seed region should supply the equations. Complete application cost determines whether the learned choice enters deployment.

The latest method verifies 58 of 58 reserved requests in every repetition. Its matched total is 134.119 seconds against 219.277 seconds for the same-basis preceding application, or 1.63 times the speed. Against the fixed batch action, the final method uses 2.5\% less time on 58 matched requests. The ratio is 0.975, with repetition interval $[0.961,0.994]$.

Against the fixed action on the forty native box requests, the final method uses 2.7\% less time on 40 matched requests. The ratio is 0.973, with repetition interval $[0.962,0.989]$. This comparison holds the exact methods and prepared basis constant.

These controls keep the sources of improvement separate. A prepared master-index set avoids repeated discovery, exact formulas avoid whole reductions, and learned choices alter the work left for native Kira. The earlier seed and equation models retain their separate measured gains. Learned row selection remains excluded because its later timing interval includes equal time.

The current result covers an explicit analytic triangle family, independent products, and two learned native graph structures. It does not demonstrate arbitrary connected thirteen-loop scattering. Every failed or unsupported request remains part of the coverage record. Further claims require new physical families, separate training or validation where needed, and complete-time comparisons with equally prepared controls.
