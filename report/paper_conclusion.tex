\section{Conclusion}
The measured decomposition time decreases when the application uses exact structure before a general equation system. Direct finite products handle the supported vacuum and propagator families, and a combined scalar one-loop recurrence handles raised powers. Independent-loop factorization extends these methods to products with many loops. Learned decisions select recurrence strategies or sparse native seed regions, while exact algebra determines the coefficients.

The combined method uses 30.2\% less time than the preceding application, with time ratio 0.698 and repetition interval $[0.676,0.717]$. The combined method uses 65.1\% less time than the matched native control, with time ratio 0.349 and repetition interval $[0.338,0.360]$. Both comparisons include the exact implementation changes. The combined method uses 4.3\% less time than the same fast implementation with fixed choices, with time ratio 0.957 and repetition interval $[0.925,0.986]$. This final control identifies the additional request-dependent learned contribution.

The new recurrence model learns measured kernel costs and must also justify its cost at the application level. The combined method uses 0.2\% more time than the fixed strategy on the nine scalar recurrence requests, with time ratio 1.002 and repetition interval $[0.959,1.076]$. The interval includes equal times. The retained seed model remains a separate learned contribution on supported coupled inputs. The row model remains omitted because its earlier live test did not resolve a complete-time gain, and the equation model retains its separately measured domain.

The initial fast-implementation comparison verifies 65 of 65 new-method requests in all repetitions. The separate twenty-second audit completes 68 of 110 requests. These results support analytic families and independent products through thirteen loops, together with the specified lower-loop native reductions. They do not establish complete reduction for every topology, numerator, or kinematic limit. Prepared master indices, training, and independent reference preparation remain separate costs. Further generality requires verified reduction methods for the coupled cases that remain incomplete.

The final deployment rule uses the fixed recurrence strategy, based on the original validation split. Its separate twelve-request comparison takes 7.254 seconds against 11.338 seconds for the preceding application, a factor of 1.56. The retained sparse-seed model remains available. This rule selects methods by complete cost while preserving the initial candidate's measurements and the broader audit limits.
