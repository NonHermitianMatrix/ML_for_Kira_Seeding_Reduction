This project developed and tested a combined approach to speeding up Feynman integral reduction with Kira. We constructed master-based training targets with exact seed and elimination records, trained and tested the original seed and row models, integrated learned choices into native equation generation, and redesigned later models around complete measured reduction cost. Exact coefficient checks and preserved failure records distinguish a useful prediction from a completed reduction.

The learned contribution is supported by specific matched tests. Equation selection saves about 6--7\% on its successful native workloads, and the shared structural model saves 4.9\% against its fixed control on 43 completed requests. Selective seed expansion and basis conversion give further gains on their stated graph set. Learned row selection is omitted because its complete-time tests do not establish a reliable retained benefit.

The mathematical contribution is larger where the integral structure permits it. Exact recurrences and shared loop dependence reduce the restricted-vacuum benchmark from 1,027.781 to 1.922 seconds, while joint processing of compatible native requests saves 48.3\%. Direct formulas and reconstruction give a separate cumulative reduction of 39.1\% on 75 mixed requests. The final 93-request comparison adds an 8.8\% saving against its preceding application. These measured gains have different controls and must remain separate.

The resulting method uses exact formulas where their assumptions hold, validated learned choices where they reduce cost, and Kira for the remaining algebra. Its analytic tests reach thirteen loops for restricted structures. General connected high-loop scattering, universal ML gains, and globally optimal reduction paths remain unestablished. The report states these limits alongside the methods and gains achieved throughout the project.
