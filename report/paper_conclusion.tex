\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
Exact subintegral identities reduce the work presented to Kira, and a learned policy selects among admissible seed settings for the remaining native problems. The solver retains responsibility for the algebra. The identities in this study cover several one-scale structures, including connected bubble trees with a massless two-point root, a one-scale triangle root, or a single massive vacuum root. We derive their target-to-master coefficients and check the new vacuum identity independently.

The latest vacuum extension completes 34 of 34 defined structures through thirteen loops. On 4 matched completed requests, total time changes from 5.046 to 0.730 seconds (Table~\ref{tab:vac-ml}). This saving comes from exact integration. The earlier cancellation of Gamma factors reduced large triangle-batch time from 14.305 to 6.731 seconds in its separate comparison (Section~\ref{sec:gamma-cancel}).

The newly trained native model takes 150.663 seconds against 156.383 seconds for the matched fixed choices. Its time ratio is 0.963, with request interval $[0.937,0.984]$. The interval describes the finite reserved collection. The final application completes a separate set of 22 requests, including two thirteen-loop batches with 128 targets each. Its complete-time ratio against the first revision is 0.964, with request interval $[0.925,0.997]$. The unchanged model completes all eight new native requests; the fixed control completes seven in every repetition. The seven matched requests give time ratio 0.963 against the final fixed choice, with interval $[0.899,0.999]$ (Table~\ref{tab:vac-ml-final}). The preceding controlled seed and equation-selection measurements remain separate evidence. Learned row selection stays outside the retained method because it has no resolved complete-time benefit. Speed factors from different collections and baselines cannot be multiplied into a universal gain.

The native build retains its 31-index input restriction, including auxiliary scalar-product coordinates. Exact integration avoids native input only for the recognized domains. The new native test concerns numerator batches in two known massless box topology classes, while the analytic tests extend through thirteen loops within the derived families. General multiscale, massive-scattering, and irreducible high-loop reductions remain unresolved. These finite comparisons establish their stated completions and timings, with no claim of exhaustive integral coverage or globally minimum reduction time.
