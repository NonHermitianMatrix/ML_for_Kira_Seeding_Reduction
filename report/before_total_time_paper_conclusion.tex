\section{Conclusion}
The method uses exact simplifications first and applies learned decisions to the native Kira work that remains. It reduces each distinct independent loop block once, retains products of master expansions in factored form, and evaluates the two convolution formulas when their physical assumptions hold. Their derivations and checks extend the specified vacuum and two-point families through thirteen loops. The derived vacuum formula gives 5.35 times faster reductions on 4 matched requests than the initial block interface; the massless formula gives 1.28 times faster reduction on 1 matched request. General coupled scattering reductions at those orders remain unverified.

The final validation selects a 48-tree extremely randomized forest with maximum depth 10. Against native choices on matched requests that invoke ML, the time ratio is 0.990, with repetition interval $[0.963,1.026]$, on 63 matched requests. Against the retuned fixed action, the time ratio is 0.991, with repetition interval $[0.972,1.011]$, on 63 matched requests. The comparison with native choices does not resolve a saving beyond repetition variation. The comparison with fixed choices does not resolve a saving beyond repetition variation. Section~\ref{sec:blocks13} gives the separate physical domains, completion counts, failed attempts, and comparison with the initial block application. These ratios measure the added learned decision with the exact changes held fixed. They do not assign analytic gains to ML.

The earlier local comparisons support learned seed and equation choices within their measured domains. The row model adds no reliable saving, so the retained method keeps native elimination rows. The shared-model holdout study and the corrected block study use different requests and controls; their ratios cannot be multiplied into a cumulative speedup.

The corrected application replaces alternative preferred master lists with one checked basis per structure. Calibration costs 255.840 seconds in this experiment, including unsuccessful preparation attempts. This cost and the historical collection of training labels remain outside the supplied-cache timing. The measurements therefore describe subsequent reductions with checked preparation and do not establish a first-use speedup for every input.

The measured benefit depends on structure and target powers. Exact checks, supported coordinates, and completed native references define the scope of each result. Unsupported inputs and incomplete calculations remain explicit, and no finite test establishes a globally fastest method for every integral accepted by Kira.

Across all timed attempts, the learned application uses 811.364 seconds, native choices use 790.074 seconds, and the fixed choice uses 818.950 seconds. This total includes the unfinished request and all repetitions. The learned application therefore does not reduce total time against native choices on this bounded workload.
