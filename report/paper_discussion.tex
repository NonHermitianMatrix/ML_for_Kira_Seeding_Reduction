\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
Our measurements show that useful learning requires a computational decision whose saved algebra exceeds the prediction and recovery costs. The seed and equation models meet that condition on several stated workloads, and cost-trained selectors improve some matched fixed strategies. The original row model does not. The constructed-data results explain why: a model can reproduce a reference path accurately while taking longer to select each step.

Exact identities provide the largest gain in the restricted-vacuum comparison because they replace many separate solves by a recurrence shared across loop orders. The same mathematical principle motivates the later analytic stage: inspect the propagators, establish that a formula applies, and reduce only the remaining unknown part. Joint target processing provides another substantial gain when requests share a family and kinematics. These are separate sources of acceleration, so their savings cannot all be assigned to ML.

The physical scope has three levels. First, the 17,100-instance constructed collection supplies known-answer targets across a small set of underlying structures. Second, native tests cover selected vacuum, two-point, and scattering families, including some symbolic-kinematic comparisons; the broad shared-model grid completes matched tests through five loops. Third, exact recurrences and formulas cover restricted independent products and connected trees through thirteen loops. A thirteen-loop product or bubble tree has algebraic structure that a general multiscale connected scattering graph may lack.

We also tested finite optimality certificates for small supplied identity pools (Appendix~\ref{app:project-optimal}). Such a certificate depends on the permitted source rows, operations, protected masters, and arithmetic-cost definition. It does not prove a globally shortest IBP reduction or a minimum elapsed time. Likewise, agreement at finite-field samples supports a basis check over the tested ranges, while exact returned coefficients and the analytic derivations provide the stronger checks used for accepted outputs.

Recovery remains a practical limit. A model can remove many equations and still lose time when the reduced system leaves additional masters. Training on complete cost, providing the required basis in the features, and retaining conservative choices reduce this risk without guaranteeing success on every unseen input. The late equation-filter failure and the slower subgroups of the final test remain in the results for this reason.

Table~\ref{tab:tradeoffs} compares the advantages and costs supported by these measurements. The distinction matters when choosing a method for a new calculation. An exact formula needs a proof that the propagators satisfy its assumptions, whereas a learned choice needs evidence that its complete cost is lower than a suitable fixed choice. Native Kira remains the general reduction stage for inputs accepted by the application when the specialized analytic conditions do not hold~\cite{KiraOriginal,Kira3}.

Together, these results support a combined procedure whose choice depends on the integral structure and measured cost. We obtained learned savings in specified native reductions, a large cumulative saving for the proved vacuum recurrence, and substantial savings from shared requests. The latest 8.8\% update is one part of that evidence. No single number represents all integral types, all loop orders, and all modes of use.
